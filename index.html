What if you copied Berkshire with $10,000?

Two paper portfolios follow the public SEC filing of Berkshire Hathaway's top five stocks. Every order is sized to a budget, checked against risk rules, and never sent to a broker.

How the plan is made

  1. Copy. Read Berkshire's quarterly 13F from SEC EDGAR and keep the top five stocks.
  2. Size. Keep a 10% cash buffer and cap any one stock at 25% of the budget.
  3. Check. A risk gate blocks orders that break the limits.
  4. Ask. A real account would need a yes from a person for every order. Here nothing is placed.

Good to know

  • A 13F shows only long US stock positions, as of the end of a quarter and filed up to 45 days later. It shows no prices paid, shorts, cash or bonds.
  • Berkshire files it as a firm. These are not Warren Buffett's personal trades.
  • Prices are delayed quotes. Gains and losses are simulated and ignore taxes and fees.
