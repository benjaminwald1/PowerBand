    How it keeps learning

    1. It writes down its odds. Every time the plan refreshes, the chance it gave each stock of hitting its profit or stop price is saved to a journal.
    2. It grades itself. Once a month, three months or a year has passed, it checks whether the price really got there and compares that with the odds it gave.
    3. It retrains on new filings. Each time Berkshire files, the history grows by a quarter, and every rule set is tested again.
    4. It only changes when it is clearly better. A new rule must beat the current one by at least a point a year on unseen years, and win most of them. Otherwise nothing changes.

            Safety rules

            • No order goes anywhere. The pipeline ends at a proposal.
            • Paper trading only. A live account would need three separate yeses: the risk check, a typed yes for each order, and the broker's own approval card.
            • 10% stays in cash, and no stock may exceed 25% of the budget.
            • A 13F shows only long US stocks, as of a quarter end, filed up to 45 days later. It is a firm's filing, not Warren Buffett's personal trades.
